# Kozani Greek tutor: inventory of existing resources

As of 20 October 2026. Stergios Chatzikyriakidis. Compiled in a Claude Code cloud session from HANDOVER.md.

## Scope and conventions

This inventory covers the Greek idiom of Kozani (Κοζανίτικα), a Northern Greek variety of Western Macedonia, and the material needed to build a language tutor for it: grammar rules and paradigms explicit enough to implement, a lexicon the tutor may legally load, example texts and audio it may legally reuse, and the existing teaching materials, orthographic conventions, regional variation, pedagogy research and NLP tools. The literature is expected to be the thinnest of the four varieties (Cypriot, Kozani, Pontic, Cretan), so the "Not found" lines carry weight.

### Which variety the tutor teaches

The core target is the town idiom of Kozani as documented for speakers born before about 1950, the variety described by Κώστας Ντίνας in Το γλωσσικό ιδίωμα της Κοζάνης (2005, two volumes, to verify in section 1). The idioms of the wider Περιφερειακή Ενότητα Κοζάνης (Σιάτιστα, Βελβεντό, Σέρβια, Βόιο, Εορδαία) and of neighbouring Γρεβενά are treated as regional variation in section 7, not as the target. Younger speakers in Kozani use a levelled regional Northern Greek with Standard Modern Greek (SMG) morphology and a reduced set of Northern vowel rules. Where a source documents that levelling, the tutor marks the older form as the dialect form and the levelled form as the current urban norm, so register decisions rest on published evidence rather than on assumption.

Key phenomena the tutor needs, in order of priority: unstressed high-vowel deletion (/i/, /u/) and unstressed mid-vowel raising (/e/ to /i/, /o/ to /u/), the consonant clusters and stress patterns that result, accusative for indirect objects (the Northern Greek dative replacement), the local lexicon with Slavic, Turkish, Aromanian and Albanian loans, and the carnival (Αποκριά, φανοί) song texts as a folk corpus.

### Items already in hand (do not re-inventory)

- GRDD and GRDD+ (Greek dialect datasets, they contain Northern Greek text alongside Cretan and Pontic).
- Newton 1972, The Generative Interpretation of Dialect, for the Northern vowel system in general.

Assumption: the author was not available at the start of this session, so no other Kozani holdings are assumed. Trudgill 2003 (Modern Greek dialects, a preliminary classification) is treated as a pointer to verify, not as in hand.

### Columns

Every table has exactly six columns in this order: Reference | Contents | Access | Reuse | Judgement | Contact.

- Reference: author, year, title in italics, publisher or venue, markdown link to the page actually opened.
- Contents: what is in it (entries, words, hours, format: print, PDF, website, XML, CSV).
- Access: Open PDF, Website, Print, Paywalled, Download, On request.
- Reuse: PD (public domain), Open (licence allows reuse with attribution, licence named), Ask (copyrighted or unclear, needs the holder's permission), No (reuse excluded by terms). A reason in parentheses where useful.
- Judgement: one short phrase on value for the tutor. Prefixed **Implementable.** when the item can be loaded directly as data or states rules explicit enough to code.
- Contact: person or institution, or [unverified].

### Reuse rule

Facts a source describes can inform the tutor's grammar without permission. Its example sentences, tables, word lists and recordings cannot. Copyright status of texts is reasoned from the author's death year plus 70 years (Greek law 2121/1993) unless a licence says otherwise.

### Verification marker

Every fact is taken from a page that was opened. A search snippet is not a source. Anything not confirmed on a primary or institutional page is marked [unverified]. No URL, size, date or licence is invented. Searches were run in Greek and English. Each section ends with one line starting "Not found:" listing what was looked for and not found.
