# Cretan Greek tutor: inventory of existing resources

Oct 20, 2026 · @Stergios Chatzikyriakidis

## Scope and conventions

This inventory covers Cretan Greek material beyond the items already in hand (GRDD and GRDD+, whose Cretan portions are the largest dialect subcorpora in both releases). The author was not available when this inventory was started, so nothing else is assumed to be in hand. In particular the holdings of the Τμήμα Φιλολογίας of the Πανεπιστήμιο Κρήτης (dialect archive, recordings, theses) are treated as unknown and are listed as an open question. Each category table uses the requested columns: reference, contents, access, reuse status, judgement, contact.

Which Cretan the tutor teaches. Three decisions, taken here so that the sections below can be read consistently.

1. The target variety is contemporary spoken Cretan of the twentieth and twenty first centuries, as used across the island by speakers of all ages. Features shared island-wide (the palatal and affricate outputs of velars before front vowels, the pronoun and article forms, the interrogative ίντα, the adverb system with εδά and επαέ, the clitic placement and augment patterns, the lexicon of everyday life) form the core. The tutor presents them as Cretan, not as western or eastern.
2. East and west are treated as marked regional layers on top of the core, following the established east vs west division of the dialectological literature (Κοντοσόπουλος). Where a source states that a form is eastern or western, the row says so and the tutor should label the form accordingly. Σφακιά and the other mountain varieties are flagged separately where a source singles them out, since they preserve forms the lowland and urban varieties have lost. Urban Cretan (Ηράκλειο, Χανιά, Ρέθυμνο) is treated as the levelled register closest to Standard Modern Greek, and the tutor's register axis runs from it to the rural basilect.
3. The literary language of the Cretan Renaissance (Ερωτόκριτος, Ερωφίλη, Θυσία του Αβραάμ, Κατζούρμπος and the rest of the corpus) is a historical register and a text source, not the target variety. Its texts are public domain and are inventoried in section 3 for reading material and for the mantinada tradition that grew out of it. Its grammar enters the tutor only as historical notes.

Reuse codes: **PD** public domain, **Open** licence allows reuse with attribution (the licence is named), **Ask** copyrighted or unclear, needs the holder's permission, **No** reuse excluded by terms. Rules and facts a source describes can inform the tutor's grammar without permission. Its example sentences, tables, word lists and recordings cannot. Copyright status of literary works is reasoned from the author's death year plus seventy years under Greek law, and the editorial apparatus of a critical edition is treated as copyrighted even where the text it edits is not.

Verification. The convention of these inventories is that every fact is taken from a page that was opened, and anything not confirmed on a primary or institutional page is marked **\[unverified\]**. This session ran in a cloud environment whose network policy allowed web search but refused every page fetch outside github.com (anemi.lib.uoc.gr, cup.gr, el.wikisource.org, greek-language.gr, wikipedia.org and doi.org were all refused on 20 October 2026). The sections below were therefore written from search results, which return page titles, URLs and extracted passages but not the opened page. Every row whose facts rest on search results alone carries the marker **\[unverified: search only\]** in its Reference cell, and the URL given is the one the search returned, not one that was opened. Rows whose facts were confirmed on a GitHub page carry no marker. The verification pass that this convention requires (open every link, confirm sizes, licences, death years and contact pages) is the first task for a session with ordinary network access, and REPORT.md lists it as such. Nothing in the tables was invented to fill a gap: where a size, year or licence was not found it is written as \[unverified\] or omitted.

Each section ends with one line starting "Not found:" listing what was looked for and not found. Greek titles are given in Greek. Searches were run in Greek and English.
