## 11. Disagreements between sources

The sources disagree most on how Cretan is written (the interrogative ίντα, είντα, ήντα), on whether velar palatalisation is island-wide and what its outputs are, and on how healthy the dialect is among the young. On syntax there is nothing to disagree about, because no study of clitic placement or negation in contemporary spoken Cretan exists. The table also records the conflicts of fact found inside this inventory (editions, licences, resource names), since each one changes a decision. Columns here: the question, the two positions with their sources, the evidence in hand, the consequence for the tutor, and how to resolve it.

| Question | Position 1 | Position 2 | Evidence in hand | Consequence for the tutor | How to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spelling of the interrogative | ίντα with iota, from τι είναι τα, ήντα and είντα wrong (Ψαθάς, Πατρίς), also the Βικιλεξικό headword (section 6) | ήντα, the form of 16th century Cretan literature (cretans.gr 2016, the Πατρίς reply), or είντα, the etymological form used by Αλεξίου's Ερωτόκριτος (section 6) | GRDD v2: είντα 2,744, ίντα 201, ήντα 29. GRDD+: 2,461, 375, 96. Dialect_Trans: ίντα 16, είντα 12. | Is velar palatalisation an island-wide rule | Crete is the one Greek area defined by this feature alone (Trudgill 2003, sections 1 and 6) | More than one type on the island, and areas without palatalisation, sometimes limited to single loanwords (Manolessou and Pantelidis, section 7) | Lengeris and Kappa 2016 (west, five speakers), Syrika et al. 2011 (Ιεράπετρα), Granqvist 1997 (Μεραμπέλλο, about 40 percent palatalised) | Treating affrication as uniform Cretan would mislabel speakers who lack it | Read the palatalisation maps in Κοντοσόπουλος's 1988 atlas and encode the rule per region, not island-wide |
| Outputs of palatalisation | One alveolo-palatal type for Crete (Trudgill 2003) | Palato-alveolar vs alveolo-palatal types, the latter eastern (Manolessou and Pantelidis), and a gradient of [c], [cç], [tɕ] conditioned by stress and position in the west (Lengeris and Kappa 2016) | Acoustic data only for five western men and twelve Ιεράπετρα and Thessaloniki adults, no released recordings (sections 1, 4, 7) | Pronunciation feedback that accepts only [tɕ] would reject attested western [c] and [cç] | Accept the gradient as variants, mark [tɕ] as the most dialectal, and test against the ILSP corpus (east) and new western recordings |
| Environment and area of the retroflex /l/ | Before back vowels and in non-prominent prosodic positions, western villages (Lengeris, Kappa, Paracheraki and Sipitanos 2014) | Before back vowels and /a/, Σφακιά and Μυλοπόταμος (arXiv 2110.03756), with Ανώγεια also cited (section 1) | Neither paper's data is released, the 2014 paper is not online (sections 1, 7) | The rule's trigger (vowel quality alone, or vowel plus weak position) and its region decide whether it belongs to the western or the mountain layer | Obtain the 2014 paper and check the atlas for /l/ maps, label the feature mountain and western until then |
| The -ε after pronouns (μας-ε, τον-ε) | An unstressed augment ε that should be kept and attached to the pronoun (Ψαθάς, Πατρίς) | A paragogic ε of the clitic, written with a hyphen as part of the pronoun (Σφακιανάκης's translations: τον-ε 892, την-ε 438, μας-ε 327) | Hyphen spelling confined to the Σφακιανάκης portion of GRDD (section 6). No grammar in the inventory analyses the form | Whether the ε belongs to the verb or the pronoun decides how the morphology module generates it | Check Πάγκαλος vol. 1 and Χαιρετάκης 2020 on pronoun forms in -ε |
| Vitality among the young | Cretan has the largest speaker group of any Greek dialect, is among the best preserved and not endangered (Τζακώστα, NoDaLiDa 2025, sections 7 and 8) | The younger the speakers the worse their attitude (Τζακώστα 2019), palatals better kept by older speakers (Granqvist 1997), rural lexicon eroding while phonology persists (Ασήμι corpus study, section 7) | Attitude surveys of teachers, pupils and students, no age-graded production study of urban speech (sections 7, 8) | Decides whether the tutor targets learners who never acquired Cretan or speakers who need register awareness | Both hold: phonology is vital, lexicon and attitudes are receding.
