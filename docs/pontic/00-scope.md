# Pontic Greek tutor: inventory of existing resources

20 October 2026 · Stergios Chatzikyriakidis

## Scope and conventions

This inventory covers Pontic Greek material beyond the items already in hand (GRDD and GRDD+, which contain Pontic text). The author was not available when the session started, so no other holdings are assumed. If he holds further Pontic material (for example the Παπαδόπουλος dictionary, the Οικονομίδης grammar or association course books), the matching rows below become "in hand" and their Access and Reuse cells are moot.

### Which Pontic the tutor teaches

Primary target: the Pontic of Greece (η ποντιακή διάλεκτος όπως διαμορφώθηκε στην Ελλάδα μετά το 1923), the koine that formed among the refugees and their descendants, mainly in Macedonia, and that the associations and their course books teach. Its written norm is the Greek script, its reference descriptions are Παπαδόπουλος (1955, 1958 to 1961), Οικονομίδης (1958) and Τομπαΐδης, and its only sizeable written corpus (theatre, poetry, song lyrics, press, the Pontic Wikipedia) is in this variety. The learners the tutor is built for are the descendants of refugees in Greece, Germany, Australia and North America, who meet this variety at home and in the associations.

Secondary, as a marked comparison module and not as a teaching target in version one: Romeyka (Ρωμέικα), the Pontic of the Muslim communities of Trabzon (Of, Çaykara, Sürmene, Tonya), documented by Mackridge (1987), Sitaridou and others. Reasons for not teaching it first: no agreed orthography, few reusable texts, speakers in Turkey, recordings held by researchers under consent terms. The tutor should still state the main contrasts explicitly where the sources allow it: the surviving infinitive, negation, the vowel inventory and Turkish contact features.

Sub-dialect labelling rule: where a source describes one sub-dialect rather than the koine, the table names it. Labels used: Τραπεζουντιακά, Χαλδιώτικα (Αργυρούπολη, Κρώμνη, Σάντα, Ίμερα), Οινουντιακά (Οινόη, Κερασούντα), Οφίτικα (the Christian variety of Of as spoken by refugee descendants in Greece), Romeyka (the Muslim variety still spoken in Turkey), Georgian Pontic (Tsalka and other settlements), Russian and Ukrainian Pontic (the Pontic of the Caucasus and the Donbas, distinct from the Rumeíka of Mariupol, which is not Pontic). Register decision for the tutor: the koine of the association courses is the default, sub-dialect forms are flagged as regional, Romeyka forms are flagged as comparison.

### Columns

Each category table uses exactly six columns in this order: Reference | Contents | Access | Reuse | Judgement | Contact.

- Reference: author, year, title in italics, publisher or venue, markdown link to the page the fact was taken from.
- Contents: what is in it (entries, words, hours, format: print, PDF, website, XML, CSV).
- Access: Open PDF, Website, Print, Paywalled, Download, On request.
- Reuse: PD, Open, Ask, No, with a reason in parentheses when useful.
- Judgement: one short phrase on value for the tutor, prefixed **Implementable.** when the item can be loaded directly as data or states rules explicit enough to code.
- Contact: person or institution, or [unverified].

### Reuse codes

**PD** public domain (in Greece and the EU, the author's death year plus 70, so authors who died in 1955 or earlier are PD in 2026). **Open** licence allows reuse with attribution, the licence is named. **Ask** copyrighted or unclear, needs the holder's permission. **No** reuse excluded by terms. Facts a source describes can inform the tutor's grammar without permission. Its example sentences, tables, word lists and recordings cannot.

### Verification marker and the limits of this session

The rule for every document in this series: every fact is taken from a page that was opened, a search snippet is not a source, and anything not confirmed on a primary or institutional page is marked [unverified].

This session ran in a cloud sandbox whose network policy refused outbound connections to every host except github.com. Web search worked, direct page opening did not (archive.org, pasithee.library.upatras.gr, academia.edu, el.wiktionary.org, pnt.wikipedia.org, zenodo.org, doi.org, cam.ac.uk, greek-language.gr and every other host tested returned a proxy denial). The sections below were therefore researched through extended web search, which reads pages on the searcher's behalf and reports their content, but not through opening the pages. The consequence for the tables:

- Rows whose facts rest on search results alone carry "[unverified: page not opened]" at the end of the Reference cell. That is most rows.
- Individual sizes, years, licences and page counts that the search results did not state explicitly are marked [unverified] on their own.
- Rows for GitHub-hosted items (datasets, code, dumps mirrored there) were opened directly and carry no marker.
- No URL in any table was invented. Every link appeared verbatim in a search result or was opened on GitHub.

The verification pass that the handover asks for (open every link, confirm sizes, licences, death years and contact pages) therefore still has to be run from a session with ordinary network access, exactly as for the Cypriot survey of sections 3 to 10. The tables are organised so that pass is mechanical: find the marker, open the link, delete the marker or correct the cell.

### Style

No em dashes or en dashes, ranges written "15 to 45". No semicolons in prose. Compact cells. Greek titles in Greek. Each section ends with one line starting "Not found:".
