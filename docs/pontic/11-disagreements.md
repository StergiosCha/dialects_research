## 11. Disagreements between sources on grammatical facts

The filled tables record fewer head-on grammatical disagreements than the Cypriot inventory did, for a reason the tables themselves make visible: the two reference grammars of the koine (Παπαδόπουλος 1955, Οικονομίδης 1958) and Drettas 1997 could not be opened, so most grammatical claims in sections 1 to 7 come from single sources. The disagreements below are the ones the tables do support, ordered by how much they matter for the tutor's design. Each names the sections whose rows carry the evidence.

**1. What the "koine" descriptions actually describe (sections 1, 7).** Glottolog tags Παπαδόπουλος 1955 and Drettas 1997 as descriptions of Χαλδιώτικα (Argyroupolis, Chaldia, Christian Pontic), while Οικονομίδης 1958 is explicitly Trapezountian with an appendix of Trebizond texts, and the Armostis et al. JIPA illustration describes Τραπεζουντιακά as spoken by second generation refugees in Etoloakarnania. The three reference descriptions therefore rest on two different sub-dialect bases. Where their paradigms differ, the tutor has no published arbiter for which form is "koine". The only empirical statement about the levelled variety is the forthcoming ILSP corpus (Konstantinidou et al. 2026), whose transcription scheme was chosen after a community questionnaire. Decision needed: adopt one base (the handover's Παπαδόπουλος pointer implies Χαλδιώτικα) and flag the Trapezountian alternatives, or wait for the ILSP data.

**2. The fronted vowels (sections 4, 6).** Every written convention in section 6 (the Παπαδόπουλος dictionary with its special glyphs, the diaeresis conventions of the three GitHub keyboard projects, the Pontic Wikipedia's εα and εο digraphs, the Turkish-Latin ö and ü of Tursun) presupposes a vowel inventory with fronted vowels. The one acoustic study of Greece-based speech, Armostis, Voniati, Drosos and Tafiadis 2020 in JIPA, found five vowels [a e i o u] in its Etoloakarnania speaker and the expected [œ] produced as [o]. The sources do not contradict each other about Pontus in 1920, they disagree about what the tutor's learners will hear and should be taught to produce in 2026. The tutor should display the diacritics (they carry lexical distinctions in the dictionaries) and treat the merged pronunciation as acceptable, not as an error, until a larger sample exists.

**3. Sub-dialect classification (sections 1, 7).** The brief's three-way division (Οινουντιακά, Τραπεζουντιακά, Χαλδιώτικα, attributed to Παπαδόπουλος and restated by Τομπαΐδης 1988) was not confirmed in any opened source and is marked [unverified]. The one classification that was opened, Glottolog pont1253, has no node for Χαλδία or Οινόη, groups Of, Sürmene, Tonya and Trapezounta under "Coastal Pontic Greek" with the alternative name "Muslim Pontic Greek", and sets Niotika apart. Drettas 1999 is reported as saying the classification is geographic rather than linguistic. For the tutor's regional flags, use Παπαδόπουλος' labels for Greece and the Glottolog labels only for Romeyka sub-varieties, as section 7 recommends, and state the source of each label.

**4. The Romeyka infinitive and negation: one side only (section 1).** The tables hold Sitaridou's account (the infinitive as a negative polarity item with Hellenistic dating, Diachronica 2014 and Lingua 2014) and Mackridge 1987's description of Of archaisms, together with Dawkins 1937's hypothesis of a medieval Asia Minor koine. No row records a published counter-position on the dating or the analysis, because none surfaced in the searches that ran. The tutor's comparison module should present the infinitive's distribution as described and avoid the diachronic claim, which is contested in the wider literature but undocumented here.

**5. Object clitics (section 1, plus items in hand).** The MA thesis on object clitics in Pontic (author not retrieved) states that Pontic object clitics are not affixal and that clitic doubling follows the Northern Greek pattern, with clitic omission and PP substitution. The author's own Dynamic Syntax treatment of Pontic clitics (Chatzikyriakidis 2010, Chatzikyriakidis and Kempson 2011, in hand and therefore not tabulated) gives a different formal account of the same positional facts. Whether the two disagree on the data (which contexts allow doubling and omission) or only on the analysis cannot be settled from the tables. Corpus frequencies for koine clitic placement, of the kind Papanicola and Armostis supply for Cypriot, do not exist for Pontic.

**6. Nominal inflection: two genitives (section 1).** Spyropoulos and Kakarikos' work on definite n-stem nouns gives the masculine -ος nouns a definite nominative in -ον (ο φίλον) and a genitive in either -ονος or -ου. The source presents both, the condition on the choice is not stated in the search results. The drill generator needs that condition or must accept both.

**7. Vitality (section 7).** Ethnologue 29, as recorded in Glottolog in March 2026, classifies Pontic as 5* Developing, not endangered. The UNESCO Atlas is listed among Glottolog's sources for Pontic and is widely cited as giving "definitely endangered" [unverified]. Section 8 will record the attitude and transmission studies behind each reading. For the tutor's framing text, cite both with their years.

**8. Dates and bibliographic facts the sources give differently (sections 1, 2, 3, 6).** Τοπχαράς' Soviet grammar: 1928 or 1932. Παπαδόπουλος 1955: 224, 228 or 248 pages. Τσοπουρίδης' dictionary: 766 or 445 pages, 22,000 or 23,000 words. Mackridge 1999: pages 25 to 30 or 101 to 105. The 2012 Οφίτικα book: Thessaloniki or Athens. Αρχείον Πόντου: 59 volumes plus 39 supplements or "over 50". The Σταυρίν tales: volume 1 (1928) or volume 12 (1946). Παρχαρίδης: 1858 to 1910 in the ΚΕΝΔΙ row of section 2, death year "not found" in section 3. None of these changes a grammar rule, all of them belong on the verification list in section 13.

**9. Orthographic encoding (section 6).** The Apple-based keyboard projects (AricSemerji, msologr-cpu) put the diaeresis on α and ο below the letter (U+0324), the fcitx5 project puts it above (U+0308). This is a disagreement between implementers, not between linguists, but it determines whether two users' input of the same word matches. The tutor must normalise both to one form and should pick U+0308, the code point the Greek Wiktionary and the Παπαδόπουλος tradition's printed look correspond to [unverified: the printed glyph was not inspected].

Not found: any published exchange in which two Pontic grammarians argue a point against each other (the Cypriot gemination and clitic debates have no Pontic counterpart in the tables), and any source that compares the paradigms of Παπαδόπουλος, Οικονομίδης and Drettas side by side.
