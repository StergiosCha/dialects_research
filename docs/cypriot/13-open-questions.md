## 13. Open questions and gaps

Grouped by who can close them: questions for the author, permission requests to send, facts still to be verified by opening pages, and resources that do not exist and would have to be built.

Decisions for the author:

- Default register: the tables support the urban Nicosia koine as default with basilectal forms as a switchable regional layer (sections 7, 11). Confirm this, and decide whether diaspora learners (UK, Australia) get the regional layer at all, given the London attitude findings (Karatsareas 2018).
- The standard for pronunciation feedback: Cypriot Standard Greek (Arvaniti 2010) or Athenian SMG. The acoustic targets differ (Themistocleous 2017, Themistocleous and Logotheti 2016).
- House spelling: adopt the Συντυχιές convention as canonical and decide what learners see (plain Greek, diacritics on request, Greeklish accepted as input). Set the rule for the negator (εν, έν or έν', section 6), since no source states one.
- Which levelled features count as koine and are taught, and which survive only regionally and are shown but not drilled. Tsiplakou 2014 and Tsiplakou and Armostis 2020 give the lists (sections 1, 7), the cut-off is a design choice.
- What the author already holds beyond the items in hand: a copy of the Συντυχιές Access database, GRDD-trained classifiers, any ΚΕΕ or Πανεπιστήμιο Κύπρου data from earlier collaborations. Section 2 says the database must be requested, confirm it is not already on disk.
- Whether the tutor needs the medieval layer at all (Μαχαιράς, Βουστρώνιος, Ασσίζες). The PD editions exist, but they are diachronic sources, not the modern register.

Resources that do not exist and would have to be built or commissioned:

- A reference grammar of the koine written for implementation. Sections 1 and 11 show the rules can be assembled from Pavlou 2018, the clitic literature, Armostis on phonetics and the levelling papers, but no single source states them, and nothing covers palatalisation or final ν for teaching.
- A machine-readable CG lexicon with paradigms. Wiktionary is small, LEXcy and ΚΟΜΟΛεξ need exports, the print dictionaries need licences and OCR.
- A CG treebank, morphological analyser, spell-checker (Orthos is experimental), text normaliser (Dimakis et al. 2025 covers Pontic only) and a tested TTS evaluation (section 9).
- A reusable spoken corpus beyond 10 Common Voice speakers: the ΚΕΕ archive and ΡΙΚ hold decades of speech under permission or paid licence, the phonetic labs hold recordings never released (section 4).
- Transcribed τσιαττιστά, TV scripts (*Βουράτε Γειτόνοι*, *Αίγια Φούξια*), theatre texts and dialect children's books (section 3).
- A CG-as-second-language pedagogy, an evaluated CALL study for any Greek dialect, and heritage-learner studies outside London (sections 5, 8).
- A dialect atlas or regional monographs, and a released quantitative variation dataset (section 7). Newton 1972 (19 maps) and Κοντοσόπουλος remain the only regional maps.
- A public catalogue or contact for the ΡΙΚ archive, a Κυπριακή Βιβλιοθήκη sound collection, and contact pages for the Εταιρεία Κυπριακών Σπουδών and the Ίδρυμα Α. Γ. Λεβέντη (section 10).

Not found: a reason to delay the tutor's first version on any of the above. Items 1 to 6 of section 12 need no permission and cover grammar, lexicon seed, texts, speech and spelling.
